Academic profile

Research

My interests lie in numerical methods for differential equations and in the mathematical analysis that helps make computational methods accurate, stable, and reliable.

Research interests

01

Numerical ODEs and PDEs

Numerical solution of ordinary and partial differential equations, including stiff problems and time-dependent models.

02

Stability, convergence, and error analysis

Understanding consistency, stability, convergence behaviour, and error bounds for numerical schemes.

03

Discretization methods

Finite-difference and finite-element ideas, high-order schemes, and meshless or generalized finite-difference methods.

04

Numerical linear algebra

Iterative methods, matrix factorizations, conditioning, and linear systems arising from discretized models.

05

Scientific machine learning

Investigating learning-based components that can complement numerical solvers while respecting mathematical structure.

06

Symbolic computation

Using symbolic algorithms to support the construction, study, and verification of numerical methods.

Proposed master's thesis direction

High-order symbolic-neural discretization schemes for stiff differential equations

Design and rigorous analysis of high-order symbolic-neural discretization schemes for stiff ODEs and PDEs, using symbolic computation. The central emphasis is on numerical analysis: consistency, stability, convergence, error behaviour, and computational validation. This is presented as a research direction, not as a completed result or published work.

Research approach

Analysis first, computation alongside

I aim to connect computational experiments with mathematical reasoning rather than treating numerical performance as the only measure of a method.

01

Mathematical foundations

Study the assumptions and properties that determine when a numerical scheme can be trusted.

02

Computational validation

Compare numerical behaviour using carefully designed test problems and reproducible experiments.

03

Responsible integration of ML

Use data-driven components where they add value, while retaining interpretability and numerical guarantees as research goals.
